about 3 years ago, micro1 lost 60% of its revenue overnight and almost went bankrupt.

today, i'm proud to share we've surpassed $1B annual run rate while remaining historically profitable.

it's been a special journey. we're grateful for every struggle, moment, and person who has been part of it.

we've taken a very untraditional path to get here. our core team is only ~180 people, spanning 14+ countries.

many of our decisions don't immediately add hundreds of millions to our run rate. some even slow us down in the short term. but we believe they compound into advantages that are impossible to replicate in the long-term.

the long term is now.

these are the 8 principles that made micro1 a leader in the data industry and will soon make us number one:

  1. human 1st AI: treat the tens of thousands of experts helping train models with absolute best-in-class care. our goal is to make AI training one of the largest, highest paying, and most enjoyable job sectors in the world
  2. U.S. first: we refuse to work with adversarial nations, or export data that improves models against the interests of Americans
  3. long-term research over short-term ARR: invest in deep research, products, and infrastructure for years from now, not just the next customer pipeline.
  4. inspiration over enforcement: no arbitrary requirements like 996. inspire people to work hard instead of forcing them. we've maintained close to 100% core team retention as a result.
  5. stay lean and global: we've reached $1B run rate with ~180 people, and intend to slow team growth significantly from here. exceptional talent is found around the world, and pedigree is the wrong thing to over-index on.
  6. maintain independence: retain full board control so we can optimize for the next several decades, remain light-weight and dynamic, and take bold risks
  7. abundant intelligence improvement: invest in building data generation models to make high-quality datasets and RL environments dramatically less expensive and more abundant.
  8. scale data on realism and horizon: realism brings the complexity of the real world into training models, while horizon extends how long models can work and how much complexity they can handle. both have enormous room to scale, and we're pushing the limits of both.
